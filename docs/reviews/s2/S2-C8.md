## security-reviewer, pass 1 (base `463bde8`)

**Verdict:** ready after fixes.

| # | Severity | Category | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | minor | authz | The delete authorizes the requirement as first loaded (the route's dependency) but not again after `_lock_live` re-reads it; only the descendants are checked on locked rows. A reporter change (now) or an approval (S2-C11) committing while the delete waits lets a former reporter, or the reporter of a just-approved requirement, delete it, cancelling its pending requests for good. Fix: `ensure` on the root after the lock, and a race test. | Fixed: `ensure(requirement.delete)` after `_lock_live`, on both paths (promote and delete); `test_a_reporters_delete_waiting_on_a_reporter_change_is_refused`, which fails without it. Siblings: restore checks the root again after its lock too (a deleted row can't be reassigned or approved, so there is no race to test); `update` and `move` authorize role-only actions, which the row's fields don't decide; `set_restriction` already checks after its lock. |

**Question (open, for the owner):** a project viewer's restore is a 403 for a number in the trash
and a 404 for one that isn't, so a viewer can tell which numbers are in the trash, though their
trash view shows nothing (DL-200: a link to a deleted item is the standard 404). The approved
spec tests require the 403.
